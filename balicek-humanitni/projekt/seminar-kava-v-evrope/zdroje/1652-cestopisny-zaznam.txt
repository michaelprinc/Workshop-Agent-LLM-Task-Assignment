Traveller's notes on London (illustrative text, not a real source)

I arrived in London and found the city full of coffee houses. The first of
them was set up, they tell me, in the year 1652 by a Greek merchant who
called his shop "the coffee man". Since then the trade has grown beyond
belief; one may meet them in every street, from the exchange to the
universities.

The custom is curious: the coffee is brewed strong and dark, and the
drinkers sit for hours discussing politics, commerce, and the news of the
continent. A single cup costs but a penny, and I confess it kept me awake
long after I had meant to sleep.

I was told that the physicians are divided about the drink — some praise its
clarity, others fear it. The ale house keepers, for their part, complain
bitterly, saying that no man drinks beer on a day when he has sat in a
coffee house.
