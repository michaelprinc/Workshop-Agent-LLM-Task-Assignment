On the coffee houses of Vienna (illustrative text, not a real source)

After the great siege was lifted in the year 1683, it is told that an
officer who had entered the city found among the captured goods a sack of
beans which his servants had taken for seed. When the beans were boiled,
the water turned black and bitter, and so the first coffee house in Vienna
was said to have been opened — with the owner learning the drink from his
own kitchen.

What is certain is that by the 1690s coffee had become a Viennese habit, and
with it a custom still kept today: because the early drinkers found the
drink too bitter, milk or sugar was added, and this milder preparation won
over those who had resisted the plain Turkish cup.

The Viennese coffee houses were more than taverns. They became places of
conversation and news, with newspapers read aloud at the tables, and they
gave their name to a whole way of spending an afternoon.

Historians still argue whether the first house was opened in 1683 or only a
few years later; the sack-of-beans story is probably legend, but it shows
how quickly the drink passed from the camp into the city.
